# 0037. A spacecraft's close-up is a true-scale fly-in; the orrery keeps exaggerated sizes

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-11-17

## Context

- The owner saw a selected craft's model drawn the Sun's size: Parker next to the Sun. #204 had
  sized the model at 0.9 of the Sun's drawn radius, so near the Sun it always matched it. His
  words: "Right now it's fakery."
- No single scale can show both. Parker is about 3 m across and the Sun 1.4 million km, a ratio
  of about 400 million, so the orrery's overview has to exaggerate sizes to show anything.
- "Look closer" (#203, ADR 0021) already gave a craft its own scene, separate from the orrery.
  It showed the model alone on a plain background, lit by studio lights.
- A design for a true-to-life view went through two Codex adversarial reviews. The first draft
  compressed distances to fit everything in one depth range. The reviews showed that this got
  phases, depth order and framing wrong from any camera but the craft's.

## Decision

The owner chose (2026-11-17) a small icon when zoomed out and, zoomed in, a view "as if you're
flying in while in a spacecraft with a camera attached to it".

- **The overview:** a selected craft draws at its icon's size, 9 px radius, at any zoom (#260).
- **The close-up:** a craft's "Look closer" with ESA DISCOS sizes is a fly-in (#261).
  - The model is drawn at its true size.
  - The Sun, the planets, the moons and 287 real stars sit at their true positions in km relative
    to the craft. three.js composes matrices in 64-bit numbers and the scene uses a logarithmic
    depth buffer, so no compression is needed. Sizes, phases (one point light at the Sun's true
    place) and occlusion are then true from any camera.
  - The camera flies in over about 4 s, from 1,000,000 km out to a few model lengths away. It
    comes in from opposite the body that looks biggest from the craft, turned 12°, so that body
    sits beside the craft in the frame.
- **The owner's other choices:**
  - real stars, from the public-domain Yale Bright Star Catalogue (V ≤ 3.5);
  - a faint fill light on the night side;
  - with reduced motion, a cut instead of the flight.
- **Size rule:** each model is scaled from DISCOS. Where DISCOS's span is at least 1.5 times the
  body, it counts arrays and booms the model also has, so the whole model is scaled to the span.
  Otherwise the body is scaled to the body.
- Satellites, and craft without DISCOS sizes, keep the studio view.
- ADR 0021 is unchanged. The close-up is still its own canvas and renderer, created only while
  it is open.

## Consequences

- The close-up is true to life. At its 2 December 2026 perihelion, 9.85 solar radii out, Parker
  flies in against a Sun 11.6° across. Juno shows Jupiter in its true phase.
- **Sizes are from DISCOS, and the models are not exact.** Measured on 2026-11-17, every model
  matched DISCOS's span or body to within 0.1 m. Where DISCOS's span is only the body (Voyager,
  Parker, Wind), the model's booms reach beyond it. That is accepted: DISCOS does not count
  booms, so it can't be checked against them.
- Positions are only as good as the sources:
  - Horizons samples, interpolated, for the craft, Earth and the moons;
  - JPL's approximate elements for the other planets.
  - A moon without samples for the date is left out, and the view names it when its planet is
    within 0.2 au.
- The close-up's lazy chunk grows: the star catalogue is about 4 kB gzipped. The first load is
  unchanged.
- **Ruled out:**
  - **True scale in the orrery itself:** nothing would be visible at the overview.
  - **Distance compression:** it gives wrong phases and depth order from a moving camera, as the
    review found.
  - **A close-up mode in `OrreryRenderer`:** see ADR 0021.
- Still to do under #261: entering the fly-in by zooming past the orrery's closest approach, with
  hysteresis, and a hand-over from the orrery's last frame. Satellites (the ISS over Earth) would
  be a later phase.
- Revisit if DISCOS sizes prove wrong for a craft, or if a browser's logarithmic depth buffer
  misdraws.

## Addendum 2026-11-17: a target bracket

The owner saw the probe "wink into existence" at the end of the flight: at its true size a craft stays under a pixel until about 300 m out. The fly-in adds a target bracket and range readout, as a camera ship would show, from the first frame, fading once the model itself is plain to see; the model and the scene are unchanged. The flight is re-timed so the last 10 km take about half of it. Details: [reference/orrery.md](../reference/orrery.md).

## Addendum 2026-11-17: zoom to enter

The owner liked entering the fly-in by zooming past the orrery's closest zoom. A wheel or trackpad gesture that starts with the camera already at its closest zoom on the selected spacecraft, and pushes on, opens the same fly-in as Look closer. The gesture that carried the camera there never crosses, because a trackpad keeps sending its momentum for a second after the fingers lift, and the rest of a crossing gesture is swallowed so it can't cut the flight short. The hand-over is the same cut as Look closer, with the flight as the transition; there is no cross-fade, so the one-context rule (ADR 0021) is unchanged. A touch-screen pinch does not enter: it sends no wheel events. Zooming out to return is not built yet. Details: [reference/orrery.md](../reference/orrery.md).

## Addendum 2026-11-18: a touch-screen pinch enters too

The 2026-11-17 addendum said a touch-screen pinch does not enter. Now it does, by the same rule: a pinch that starts with the camera already at its closest zoom on the selected spacecraft, and spreads on, opens the fly-in; the pinch that carried the camera there never does. A pinch has a clear start and end, so it needs no timing. It depends on a pinch keeping the camera on the craft, which it did not before #264. Zooming out to return is still not built. Details: [reference/orrery.md](../reference/orrery.md).
