Dwarf planets

Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Makemake and Haumea, placed by JPL Horizons. Pluto has data for the whole clock; the other four from 1600 to 2500, and outside that only their orbits are drawn. Choose Pluto to see Charon

Comets, asteroids & interstellar

Comets

The comets JPL forecasts brightest tonight. Each is placed for ±30 days of today; its orbit line is drawn from its orbital elements

Bright asteroids

The asteroids JPL’s H and G estimate to be brightest at the last refresh (about magnitude 11.5 or brighter); Ceres is drawn only by the Dwarf planets switch, though the Sky view lists it. Each is placed from daily JPL samples for about a week around the refresh and is hidden outside it; its orbit line is drawn from its orbital elements

Interstellar objects

ʻOumuamua, Borisov and 3I/ATLAS: visitors from other stars, on paths the Sun cannot hold

Near-Earth objects

Asteroids passing within 0.05 au (19 lunar distances) of Earth. The data covers ±30 days of today; closest approach is counted from the date shown.

Hazardous only

JPL's “potentially hazardous” list: bigger than about 140 m, on an orbit that comes within 0.05 au of Earth's. A label, not a prediction of a hit.

On ESA's risk list only

ESA's list of objects whose orbit, as known today, leaves a tiny modelled chance of a hit within 100 years. Most drop off as observations come in.
